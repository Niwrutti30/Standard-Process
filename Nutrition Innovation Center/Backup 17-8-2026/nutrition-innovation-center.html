Nutrition Innovation Center

A new model for nutrition research

Whole food nutrition demands methods that respect its complexity. At the Nutrition Innovation Center, we’re applying systems biology, food synergy, and translational science to examine whole food matrices as integrated systems rather than isolated inputs.

Our work spans soil regeneration, nutrients density, formulation behavior, and physiological response. We follow the question wherever it leads, from the field to the cell. The aim is not to validate assumptions, but to build frameworks that better explain how whole food-based supplements function in living systems.

Areas of research

Food synergy in action

Food synergy examines how nutrients and bioactives interact within whole food matrices to influence biological outcomes. Rather than assuming a single driver of effect, this research evaluates combined activity, absorption behavior and matrix-dependent performance within living systems.

Multi-nutrients effects

Biological systems respond to combinations, not just isolated inputs. This research evaluates how multiple nutrients and phytoactives influence detoxification, methylation, immune modulation and other pathways through coordinated activity rather than single-mechanism intervention.

Biological network mapping

Using sytems biology tools, researchers example how whole food based supplements influence interconnected biological network. Multi-omics analysis, electrophysiology and biomarker mapping reveal system-level responses that cannot be captured through reductionist models alone.

Bridging dietary and mechanistic science

Bridging dietary and mechanistic science This work connects food-like extracts to measurable molecular and metabolic outcomes. By linking agricultural inputs and formulation decisions to mechanistic findings, research strengthens the biological plausibility behind whole food-based supplement design.

Whole food complexes vs isolates

Whole food complexes vs isolates Research compares intact whole food matrices with isolated compounds to evaluate differences in absorption, utilization and systemic response. These investigations help clarify how nutrients context influences biological performance.

Preserving food integrity

Preserving food integrity Transformation from crop to supplement introduces risk to nutrients complexity. Research evaluates how harvest timing, processing methods and formulation choices influence matrix preservation, bioactivity and functional outcomes.

Excipient innovation

Excipient innovation Excipients enable supplement formulation and production, yet many are chemically processed and biologically inert. This research explores whole food–based fillers, emulsifiers, binders and lubricants derived from farm-grown ingredients, with the aim of supporting formulation performance while preserving nutrients integrity.

A healthy diet helps transition individuals towards numerous long-term metabolic benefits but is challenging to maintain. Supplementation with additional whole food-based concentrates and herbs may support this shift by enhancing metabolic resilience. This randomized, controlled, crossover study evaluated a 21-day purification program emphasizing plant foods and whole food supplementation on metabolic detoxification, oxidative stress, and gut microbiota composition. Participants followed a plant-based diet for 7 days, with lean proteins introduced from days 8–21. Whole food supplements rich in polyphenols, glucosinolates, and prebiotic fibers were included to support metabolic and gastrointestinal function.

There is a growing interest in the impact of omega-3 fatty acids [i.e., eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)] on the health of dogs. The purpose of this study was to understand the effects of EPA + DHA supplementation in dogs on, first, the erythrocyte EPA + DHA level (i.e., the Omega-3 Index) and, second, health-related outcomes. We found that supplementation significantly improved the Omega-3 Index in all dogs, pain scores in small and medium-sized dogs, and quality of life scores in smaller dogs. Some of these effects may have been dose-related. Overall, our findings support the hypothesis that increased EPA + DHA intake in dogs fed standard commercial diets may provide health benefits, but further research is needed to define target Omega-3 Index levels associated with specific health conditions and optimal doses.

Lameness and restricted mobility are a significant concern in companion animals experiencing chronic pain, inflammation, or age-related pathologies. The growing awareness of health risks and side effects associated with the long-term use of prescription analgesics requires different management strategies to address these issues. In this study, we conducted a crossover evaluation of the effect of full spectrum hemp oil dosed orally at 2 mg/kg BID phytocannabinoids for 8 weeks in dogs (n = 37) living with pain in common household settings.

Canine renal insufficiency is often managed with a low phosphorus and protein diet that is not ideal for long-term use. This strategy is effective for its purpose of slowing progression but does not address the root cause of the renal burden. In this case, specific dietary modifications and supplementation were utilized to achieve the overall health of the animal including renal and gastric support. Upon initial presentation, the canine patient was in pain due to Intervertebral Disc Disease (IVDD) and was immediately prescribed a Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID) to ameliorate pain. Soon after NSAID administration, the patient was showing clinical signs of renal insufficiency (e.g. adipsia, panting, shaking) as well as elevated creatinine and Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) values. The use of functional whole foods and supplementation under veterinary supervision allowed discontinuation of NSAID use and recovery of renal burden as opposed to utilizing a commercial therapeutic diet.
